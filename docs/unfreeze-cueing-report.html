Conclusions

Two cues address different situations. The chest camera can trigger a rhythmic sound on approach to a doorway. A separate movement detector can trigger a thigh cue during suspected freezing. The competition demonstration can show each trigger independently.Prevention at doorways, reliable prediction before onset and recovery with thigh stimulation each require their own validation.

On-demand auditory cues have the closest clinical evidence

Clinical guidance supports external cueing in Parkinson’s physical therapy. The 63-participant DeFOG randomized trial found an immediate benefit while using the device. Its sound followed detected freezing; applying sound before crossing a doorway is a separate hypothesis. [1] [5]

The thigh is a candidate site for testing perception

In a seated study, participants with Parkinson’s disease identified thigh stimulus location correctly in 96% of trials. A separate small study tested lower-leg tendon vibration after freezing. Gait outcomes with this thigh-mounted device remain open. [9] [10]

Doorways can be detected visually. Preventive cueing needs a separate test.

Why doorways? Freezing of gait is a brief interruption or marked reduction of forward stepping despite the intention to walk. A doorway experiment found stride changes and increased variability on approach in people who freeze. The openings were approximately 0.675, 0.90 and 1.80 m wide. Those experimental widths do not establish a clinical alarm threshold. In a survey of 171 respondents with freezing, 69% reported doorway-related freezing. Lighting, clutter, automatic doors and space on either side also mattered. [2] [3]

Motion during freezing and prediction before onset are separate tasks

Freeze · Detection during an episode

In a 2022 offline study of 12 participants, the waist plus left thigh configuration reported sensitivity78.9%, specificity91.3% and precision46.4%. This supports investigating two sensors and illustrates how overall accuracy can conceal false positives. These results apply to the published dataset and evaluation. [7]

Pre-FoG · Prediction before onset

Palmerini et al. retrospectively studied 11 participants who froze using both ankles and the lower back, with labels covering two seconds before onset. Mean cross-participant AUC was approximately0.76. A different sensor layout needs independent validation, especially for akinetic freezing, slow walking, voluntary stops and turns. [8]

  1. Placement. In a two-IMU experiment, a lateral thigh sensor can capture leg swing while a lower-back sensor captures trunk motion. Mount a thigh actuator separately from the IMU and measure interference during vibration. The submitted v4 classifier uses one primary lower-back IMU.
  2. Data. Daphnet provides thigh and trunk acceleration for replay, with freezing, nonfreezing and invalid periods. Pre-FoG labels would have to be derived; gyroscope features require another training source. [12]
  3. Evaluation. Use labeled in-episode windows for detection. For prediction, end each input window before onset and report actual warning time separately from the two-second labeling interval. Split by participant and recording before constructing overlapping windows. Include voluntary stops, turns, starts, stamping, waiting and actuator-on periods when measuring false alarms. [8] [12]

The Parkinson’s group correctly localized anterior thigh and wrist stimulation in 96% and 92% of trials. The site difference was not significant. [9]The thigh is a plausible cue location. The 96% measures localization; recovery of gait was a different outcome.
Vibration on the less affected side after onsetIn a study of 16 people, researchers applied triceps surae tendon vibration at the first freeze. The first episode was shorter with less-affected-side stimulation (p = 0.038). Continued stimulation did not significantly prevent subsequent episodes. [10]This motivates a reactive demonstration. Moving to thigh skin and automatic detection changes the stimulation site, mechanism and timing.
180 Hz、250 msA torso study reported vibration perception at 180 Hz and a minimum perceived interval of 250 ms. A 2025 thigh study used motors calibrated to 180 Hz for static perception without comparing frequencies. [9] [13]These are bench references. The suggested 80–250 Hz range and the 250 ms interval do not establish an optimal thigh carrier or pulse duration.
Can vibration improve freezing?A 31-participant vibrating-sock study compared uncued walking, auditory cues and two tactile conditions. The group showed no significant reduction in percentage of time frozen, and individual responses varied. [11]First verify timely, recognizable output. Assess clinical outcomes for the specific person, conditions and device.

Carrier frequency and cue tempo. Carrier frequency in hertz describes vibration inside a pulse. Tempo in beats per minute describes the spacing of pulses: 100 BPM gives 600 ms between starts. Pulse duration and drive level are independent settings. Actuator and driver characteristics determine the actual carrier at the skin. A PWM setting alone cannot establish it. [9] [17]

Proposed sensing, cueing and stopping sequence

Visual gating

Retain the UNO Q video detection Brick. Box center and stability help filter side doors and fleeting detections. Increasing box size could be investigated as an approach cue. A range reading from the wall beyond a door cannot establish its clear width. Rearm after leaving the qualifying view. [14]

Local buzzer and thigh motor

Linux runs inference and sends cue parameters and stop requests to the MCU over Bridge. The MCU times each output. Use the module buzzer for beats and a phone or suitable speaker for tracks. Match the thigh actuator to its driver and power supply. [15] [17]

Customizable phone audio

Use foreground LAN events for the demo. Keep the phone awake, audition the selected track and preload a clear first beat. Begin local playback oncue_start and stop oncue_stop. Deduplicate by cue identifier and record the actual first beat. [14] [16]

Network loss and screen lock

Use the board buzzer as fallback and discard stale cues after reconnect. Background or lock-screen playback would require platform-specific testing. Android Media3 documents a possible future implementation. [18]

Verify when the cue reaches the user

Existing vision result. The team's Edge Impulse INT8 validation screenshot shows mAP@50 of 0.90, mAP@75 of 0.67 and overall mAP of 0.54. Images came from two videos of one vestibule. Measure opening-width error, performance at new sites and end-to-end timing separately. [19]

Presentation notes

Six slides for a four-minute demonstration. Reserve slide five for measured footage, screenshots and timing. The sequence diagram and study chart can be redrawn in PowerPoint.

  1. At a doorway, a person intends to walk but cannot take the next step

    Use a chest-view doorway image. The 69% survey figure refers to 171 respondents who already reported freezing. [3]

  2. Rhythm on approach, tactile cue on suspected freezing

    Show approach, suspected freezing and recovery on a timeline. Mark pre-onset prediction as experimental.

  3. Three findings that shaped the design

    The route study counted 59 versus 14 episodes; the DeFOG trial enrolled 63; thigh localization was 96%. Label these as continuous rhythm, reactive audio and seated perception. They measure different outcomes. [4] [5] [9]

  4. On-board inference and selectable phone tracks

    Draw the camera and primary IMU feeding the UNO Q. Show Bridge, MCU timing and the three outputs. Mark a second IMU as an experimental comparison channel.

  5. Demonstrate three scenarios

    Approach a door, pass a door sideways and replay a labeled movement sample. Annotate the first box, audible beat and vibration from a live recording or measured log.

  6. What has been tested and what comes next

    Report the same-site INT8 mAP@50 of 0.90 and only route measurements the team actually recorded. New settings, prediction before onset and clinical benefit remain separate evaluation tasks. [19]

Suggested wording for questions

Unfreeze detects a stable doorway in front of the camera and can provide an audible beat. A separate on-board motion classifier estimates freezing from lower-back acceleration and can trigger thigh vibration. Labeled replay checks the decision logic. Evaluation at new doorways and measurement of physical and clinical effects are separate tasks.

Presentation closing line:Unfreeze delivers a rhythmic audio cue on approach to a doorway, and an on-demand thigh haptic cue when the motion model flags a possible freeze.
